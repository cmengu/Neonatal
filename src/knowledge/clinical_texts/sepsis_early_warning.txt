[Source: HERO-GM-2001; NG-METHOD] The abnormal heart-rate-characteristics (increased-risk) signature in premature neonates consists of three concurrent changes that can appear 12-24 hours before overt clinical signs. First, sustained reduction in short-term variability: RMSSD drops below the patient's personal 2-standard-deviation threshold. Second, a shift consistent with reduced parasympathetic tone. Third, increased bradycardia frequency — recurrent episodes. When these co-occur the pattern is an adjunct risk trend warranting closer clinician review, not a sepsis diagnosis; empirical performance on this cohort is unmeasured (no sepsis labels).
Category: sepsis_early_warning. Risk tier: RED.

[Source: HERO-GM-2001; AAP-TERM-2018] The autonomic changes that can precede clinical deterioration in neonates tend to follow a temporal pattern. Parasympathetic withdrawal is often visible first, typically 18-24 hours before fever, elevated CRP, or a culture-positive blood draw, manifesting as declining RMSSD and pNN50. A neonate showing a sustained personalised RMSSD z-score of -2.5 or worse, especially with recurrent bradycardia, should be flagged for clinician review and considered for blood culture — a clinician decision, since laboratory tests alone are neither sensitive nor specific for early-onset sepsis (AAP).
Category: sepsis_early_warning. Risk tier: RED.

[Source: HERO-GM-2001; NG-METHOD] Recurrent bradycardia with HRV suppression is a distinct pattern from isolated bradycardia. Isolated bradycardia in a premature neonate may reflect normal vagal reflexes. However, bradycardia events occurring alongside suppressed RMSSD (z-score below -2.0) represent abnormal autonomic characteristics. Three or more bradycardia events in a 6-hour window accompanied by HRV suppression warrants prompt clinical evaluation regardless of other vital signs.
Category: sepsis_early_warning. Risk tier: RED.

[Source: HERO-GM-2001; NG-METHOD] Early clinical deterioration in neonates under 32 weeks gestation presents differently from older infants. Temperature instability may be absent or show hypothermia rather than fever. CRP elevation lags HRV changes by 12-18 hours, and blood cultures may be negative at the point when HRV changes are most pronounced. This is why HRV monitoring targets a detection window that preclinical laboratory markers cannot — as an adjunct trigger for clinician assessment. The personalised baseline approach matters in this gestational-age group because population-average thresholds miss the 30-40% of infants whose individual normal range falls outside population norms.
Category: sepsis_early_warning. Risk tier: RED.
